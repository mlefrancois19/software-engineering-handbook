# 03 — Meetings & Team Rituals

Meetings are where remote teams waste the most time — or save the most. The rule that governs this chapter: **sync time is a budget, not a default.** Agenda in the invite, written outcome after the meeting, or it does not happen. Chapter 05 inventories the ceremony *shapes* (Scrum/Kanban/Scrumban); this chapter is how they *run* when nobody is in the room.

## Meeting policy

- **Default: no meeting.** Before creating one, ask three questions: *Can this be a doc? Can this be async (comments/PR)? Who actually needs to be here?* If the first or second answer is yes, the meeting does not exist.
- A meeting happens only if it has **both** a written agenda in the invite **and** a written outcome promised (decision, action items, or summary). A no-agenda invite is a cancellation waiting to happen.
- **Status never meets.** Status is written: ticket, board, or a short update (the format lives in chapter 02). A status meeting is a documentation failure wearing a calendar entry.
- Minutes, recordings, and summaries are the meeting's *product* — without them the meeting did not happen.

## What deserves sync time

| Meeting type | Verdict | Why |
|--------------|---------|-----|
| Kickoffs | Sync, saved by async prep | Alignment on intent before work begins |
| Design discussions that stalled in writing | Sync | Ambiguity that survived a doc needs live reasoning |
| Incident coordination | Sync | Speed and shared situation awareness (chapter 12) |
| Retrospectives | Hybrid | Written prep, facilitated live discussion |
| 1:1s (manager, skip-level) | Sync | Relationship, feedback, career — never async-able |
| Hiring debriefs | Sync | Evidence weighing needs live discussion (chapter 14) |
| Status updates / reporting | Never | Write it (chapter 02) |
| Most design/architecture reviews | Async | Comment window over live read-aloud (chapters 04, 07, 08) |
| Decision announcements | Never | The decision record is the announcement (chapter 04) |

## Rituals adapted to remote

### Standup
Becomes a **written update** (chapter 02's format) or an async board check — the board *is* the standup (chapter 04). A synchronous standup is only justified for a genuinely stuck team, and then it is a problem-solving session, not a status round: three people who need each other live, not nine people narrating tickets.

### Planning
Async prep (written priorities and capacity — chapters 04, 05) followed by a short sync to agree. Never read a backlog aloud in a meeting. The sync is for trade-offs and commitment, not for transcription.

### Retrospective
Deadliest to lose, easiest to degrade, and the one ritual that must survive (chapter 05's kanban note applies here: without a time-box, retros quietly die).

1. **Written brainstorm first**: everyone contributes async — start/stop/continue, or 4L (liked/learned/lacked/longed for) — in their own time zone, before the session. No one is railroaded by the loudest voice at 9am.
2. **Facilitated sync**: cluster the contributions, discuss, pick the top items.
3. **Every action item becomes a tracked ticket** with an owner (chapters 04, 12). A retro with no tickets is a complaint session.

### Demo / showcase
A **recorded walkthrough** with a written summary and an async comment thread is the default; live sessions only where they add something the recording cannot. The recording is the artifact of record — a demo that exists only live did not happen.

### 1:1s
Deliberately scheduled — manager and skip-level — and protected. The agenda belongs to the report. The meeting that always gets canceled is the one that mattered; rescheduling a 1:1 casually is how remote teams lose their early-warning system. Lightweight written notes, no surveillance.

## Meeting hygiene

- **Agenda in the invite or the meeting is canceled.** Enforced without exception removes 80% of bad meetings instantly.
- **One notes taker; recording + transcript + written summary** posted to the channel after. The summary is the deliverable; the meeting is the event.
- **Decision capture**: every meeting ends with "what was decided, by whom, recorded where" (chapter 04). If nothing was decided, say that too.
- **25/50-minute honesty**: book the time the meeting needs. Address an hour of status in 25 minutes, or cut it.
- **Attendee cap**: no tourists. A person without a speaking role gets the summary; their time is worth more in the ticket queue.
- **No-meeting windows** (e.g., afternoons) and meeting-free days protect deep work. Enforced team-wide — one person's calendar cannot defend itself.
- **Timezone rotation**: recurring meetings rotate across the team's zones so the same person isn't always at 7am. A meeting that cannot rotate becomes a written update (chapter 02) or dies.
- **Cancel early, without guilt.** An agenda that no longer applies is a canceled meeting.
- **Quarterly meeting audit**: same discipline as alert hygiene (chapter 12) — review every recurring meeting, delete the zombies, merge the duplicates, ask "what happened last time this meeting was skipped?"

## Virtual team building

- Culture is built **first through work artifacts**: PRs with a human tone, reviews that teach, docs that credit their authors (chapter 09). The work itself is the social glue; social events are a supplement, never the foundation.
- Low-key, optional scaffolding: coffee-chat pairings, no-agenda pairing sessions, an async social channel run at low noise. **Never force it** — mandatory fun is resentment with a calendar invite.
- Remote hires get their social context the same way: through visible work, generous reviews, and invitation into discussions (chapters 09, 14).

## Anti-patterns

- ❌ The sync status meeting that could be a doc.
- ❌ Meetings without a written outcome — including "we discussed it, nothing was decided."
- ❌ Zombie recurring meetings nobody questions (audit them quarterly).
- ❌ Timezone-hostile scheduling that always favors one zone.
- ❌ Back-to-back meeting days that leave no contiguous deep-work block.
- ❌ "Let's jump on a call" for everything — a meeting is the escalation, not the default (chapter 02).
- ❌ Meetings as a substitute for writing — the #1 remote failure mode (chapter 01). Stale docs produce more meetings; better docs produce fewer.
